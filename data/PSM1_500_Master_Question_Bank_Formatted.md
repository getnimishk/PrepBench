### Question 001
[Topic: Empiricism]
[Type: single_choice]
[Difficulty: medium]
[Domain: Domain 1: Understanding & Applying Scrum Framework]
[Focus Area: Empiricism]
[Tier: Tier 2: Applied Situational]

A healthcare organization is developing a patient scheduling platform. Midway through the Sprint, a regulatory change invalidates a planned Product Backlog item. The Product Owner suggests adding a replacement item that supports the Sprint Goal. The Developers confirm they can complete it without endangering the Sprint Goal. What should happen?

- [x] Update the Sprint Backlog collaboratively and continue pursuing the Sprint Goal.
- [ ] Reject the modification because Sprint scope is locked during Sprint Planning.
- [ ] Immediately cancel the Sprint and schedule an emergency Sprint Planning session.
- [ ] Complete the original item anyway because it was committed during Sprint Planning.

**Correct Answer:** A

**Explanation:**
The Sprint Backlog is a plan that can be adapted throughout the Sprint as more is learned, provided the Sprint Goal is not endangered.

**Why the others are wrong:**
- **B:** Sprint scope is negotiable between the Product Owner and Developers as new information emerges.
- **C:** Cancellation is reserved for when the Sprint Goal itself becomes obsolete, not a single item swap.
- **D:** Delivering an invalidated item wastes effort and contradicts empirical adaptation.
